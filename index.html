Result 01 · Divergence time

The standard metric sees nothing.

Ask "is the state in the right place at the right time?" — and all four models fail at statistically identical rates, in every chaos bin, under both integrators. Chaos guarantees it. The confidence intervals overlap completely.

Median divergence time · steps · RK4

Whiskers = 95% bootstrap CI (2,000 resamples) · threshold sensitivity 0.1–0.9 preserves the null · Euler tells the identical story, ruling out integrator artifacts.

Why this contradicts the literature

Prior "HNN tracks longer" results were confounded.

Earlier comparisons — including this study's own preliminary runs — gave the HNN a different backbone than the MLP. Match the architectures exactly and the trajectory-tracking advantage vanishes.

What survives the matching is something the temporal metric was never able to see ↓

✗ Divergence time alone would conclude: "HNNs are useless here."

Result 02 · Hausdorff distance

The orbit remembers its shape.

Hausdorff distance is phase-independent: two trajectories visiting the same points at different times score zero. Ask "did you visit the right places at any time?" — and the HNN wins massively, with the margin growing as chaos intensifies.

The mechanism, physically

Two ways to fail a chaotic system.

Phase error: on the correct orbit, at the wrong time. Geometric error: off the orbit entirely — and once off the energy surface, every next prediction is conditioned on an unphysical state. Recovery is impossible.

The HNN's dynamics come from autograd of a learned scalar H(q,p), so its trajectories live on level sets of H. It can only fail the first way. The MLP fails both.

✓ "Right place, wrong time" is the optimal failure mode: phase can be corrected downstream; geometry loss is catastrophic.

Result 03 · Decomposition & chaos scaling

Second-order helps a fixed amount.
The Hamiltonian is what scales.

Direct contradiction · Gruver et al., ICLR 2022

"Second-order structure suffices" is regime-dependent.

Gruver et al. showed a MechanicsNN — knowing only q̇ = v — matches HNNs on pendulums and mass-spring systems. Correct there. False in chaos.

Isolating the Hamiltonian-specific gain (MechanicsNN → HNN) shows it more than doubles from low to high chaos, while the second-order part stays roughly constant.

05
The paper

Read it. Cite it. Reproduce it.

Abstract — Hamiltonian Neural Networks impose energy conservation as a structural prior, yet the literature has not identified which component of this prior drives their advantage, nor whether that advantage scales with chaos intensity. This study addresses both questions in the gravitational three-body problem by training four architecturally matched models spanning a spectrum of physics structure: a standard MLP, a MechanicsNN that knows only that the system is second-order, an MLP with energy regularization (MLP+E), and a full HNN — all sharing an identical ResBlock backbone with ~530K parameters. Evaluation on 454 held-out systems, stratified by maximal Lyapunov exponent, reveals a result that contradicts the standard narrative: trajectory divergence time is statistically identical across all four models, in every chaos bin, under both Euler and RK4 integration. The HNN advantage manifests entirely in orbit geometry — Hausdorff distance reductions of 39%, 66%, and 68% (p < 10⁻⁶). Decomposition shows second-order structure accounts for roughly half the geometric improvement in low chaos but only a quarter in high chaos: the Hamiltonian constraint specifically is what scales with chaos (Spearman ρ = 0.246). Soft energy regularization provides no benefit beyond second-order structure, while the structural constraint cannot be optimized away. HNN errors manifest as phase shifts along the correct orbit; standard network errors disrupt the orbit shape entirely. The mechanism of HNN advantage is geometric, not temporal — and it strengthens precisely where standard methods fail most catastrophically.
